# Capacity & Scheduling Audit: diagnosis

Journey: available qualified resources → feasible slot → accepted workload → travel/preparation → delivered work → capacity review.

Define the planning horizon and resource unit: qualified crew hours, specialist slots, equipment or deliverable jobs by class. Headcount and open calendar hours are not automatically productive capacity. Include travel, preparation, admin, owner-approved breaks/availability, expected job duration ranges, skills, equipment, geography and dependencies. Multiple technicians on one job consume multiple resource hours; do not treat crew hours and technician hours as interchangeable.

Separate accepted obligations, tentative work, active backlog, reschedules, emergency reserve and optional new demand. Check mix and uncertainty rather than applying a universal utilization target. Do not infer maximum capacity from an unusually good day or recommend overload. Service-level or urgent escalation commitments come from the business; do not invent safety rules, employee availability or arrival guarantees. Improving schedule fit may be more useful than increasing lead volume.

Separate symptom, cause hypothesis, alternative explanation and validation step. Labels and limitations belong with findings. Use anonymized examples/aggregates where possible; no credentials or unnecessary customer information. A brief focused report is preferable to repeating unrelated funnel questions.
