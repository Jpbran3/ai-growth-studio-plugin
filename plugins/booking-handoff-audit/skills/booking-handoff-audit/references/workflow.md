# Progressive diagnosis

Build only the evidence needed to choose the next action. Ask what happens with a recent representative inquiry, who qualifies it, who offers a slot, how confirmation is recorded and who accepts responsibility after booking. Owner answers are estimates unless records can actually be inspected.

Useful branches:
- Customers abandon before a slot: distinguish unclear service fit, price expectations, intake burden, inconvenient availability and slow ownership.
- Office books but dispatch cannot deliver: check shared availability, qualifications, travel, equipment and acceptance of the handoff.
- Technician lacks essential context: find the minimum useful job details and acknowledgement step; avoid collecting unnecessary personal information.
- Repeated reschedules: check whether slots were tentative, customer constraints changed, emergencies displaced work or overbooking occurred.
- No-shows: distinguish incorrect confirmation details, customer choice and process failures; messages alone do not prove prevention.
- Everything is working: report supported strengths and maintain a lightweight measurement routine. Do not invent a leak to justify a product.

Offer a small supported interpretation between questions. Example: “The slot appears to be promised before dispatch checks availability. That makes acceptance of the handoff the next place to inspect.” Phrase unverified causes as hypotheses.

A public website is optional. If supplied, inspect relevant service/booking promises and branding when tools permit. Cite inspected pages; disclose access limits. Do not claim the booking system was tested from public copy alone. Minimize customer identifiers; anonymized records are usually sufficient.
