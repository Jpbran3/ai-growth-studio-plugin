# Conversational workflow

## Opening

If details are missing: “Let's follow what happens to a lead and find the gaps. What's your business name, what kind of home service work do you do, and do you have a website? It's fine if you don't know your numbers.” Ask only missing identity details. If the user already provides sufficient operating data, skip intake and analyze it.

If they lead with ad spend: acknowledge that spend alone cannot show whether the problem is lead quality, handling or job conversion. Do not call the spend wasted without source-to-job evidence. Offer that insight immediately, then gather identity/context.

## Follow one representative lead

Use the journey, not a fixed questionnaire. Select the next question that could change the recommendation.

| Point | Useful question | Early value |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival | Where do new inquiries come from, and roughly how many in a typical week or month? | Establish volume and distinguish inquiries from unique new leads. |
| Contact | Who handles calls/forms when the team is busy or closed? | Locate coverage and ownership gaps; no loss claim yet. |
| Speed | What happened to the last missed call or web inquiry, and how long until a human response? | Separate automatic acknowledgment from actual contact. |
| Follow-up | If they don't answer or don't decide, what happens next and who owns it? | Spot unowned work or a one-attempt process. |
| Booking | How do they get an appointment/estimate, and are suitable slots available? | Distinguish demand, qualification and capacity. |
| Fulfillment | Does the booked visit actually happen? Why do visits cancel? | Separate preventable friction from customer choice. |
| Closing | Of completed estimates, how many become accepted jobs, and what reasons do customers give? | Avoid assuming every unaccepted estimate is recoverable. |
| Existing customers | Is repeat service relevant, and is there an appropriate follow-up process? | Keep future retention separate from new-lead losses. |

Keep each intake reply to one brief insight and the next useful question, normally 25–50 words. For example: “One evening callback leaves some inquiries unresolved. Do you try again or send a text?” Ask one question by default; pair two only when closely related and easy to answer. Do not repeat the business name, summarize every prior answer, deliver mini-reports, or explain every caveat each turn. Keep uncertainty in concise wording such as “possible gap,” and preserve the full evidence ledger for the report. If an answer adds no evidence, move to the next useful question without manufacturing an insight.

## Helping estimate

Explain “missed-call rate” as calls that weren't answered divided by inbound calls in the same period. Suggest checking one recent representative week of call logs, counting new inquiries separately. If the owner recalls 2–4 missed inquiries per working day over 20 days, label 40–80/month an OWNER ESTIMATE; confirm the number of working days rather than assuming it. Do not extrapolate a holiday, storm or seasonal peak as a normal month without a caveat.

For typical job value, use collected/invoiced revenue per comparable completed job, not the owner's largest project or profit. Separate repair, replacement, projects and recurring visits where their values differ. If the owner cannot estimate, keep counts and qualitative impact; no made-up dollar headline.

An owner agreeing to an illustrative assumption does not make it measured data. Keep it ASSUMED. Record the basis, period, cohort and revision history in the state contract.

## Special cases

- Contradiction: repeat the conflicting figures and ask what each counts. Do not silently choose one. If 80 estimates and 90 jobs mix prior-period work or direct service calls, separate cohorts; until clarified withhold a shared funnel rate.
- Solo operator: use realistic call windows, overflow arrangements, clear voicemail and an assigned callback habit. Never suggest unsafe phone answering during work/driving.
- Larger company: investigate per-branch/channel/shift ownership, transfer failures and handoff accountability. Aggregate figures can hide local gaps.
- Multiple trades: split journeys, values and conversion denominators when materially different. Never pool $200 cleaning visits with $20,000 remodels to inflate opportunity.
- Minimal data/repeated “I don't know”: use a recent real example and a small measurement plan. Avoid repeated pressure or turning illustrative guesses into facts.
- Capacity constrained: improved lead conversion may mean better selection, waitlist handling or less acquisition spend, not more deliverable revenue.
- Abandonment: each turn leaves a useful insight. Upon “just give me what you have,” deliver a preliminary audit, avoid invented totals and do not gate it on more answers.

## Stopping rule

A useful audit needs an understandable journey, at least one supported gap or a clear finding that no gap is established, likely causes labeled by evidence, a feasible next action and an honest account of uncertainty. Revenue quantification is optional. If further questions would not alter priorities, stop intake and create the report files using [report delivery](report-delivery.md). State what could change the conclusion in the document. In chat, return the report links and the main takeaway in a few sentences; do not paste the full audit.
