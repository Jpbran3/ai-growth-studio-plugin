# Evidence, confidence and prioritization

## Numeric labels

KNOWN: directly supported by accessible business records for a specified period/cohort. Owner stating “our report shows 300” without sharing the report is ESTIMATED / OWNER-REPORTED, with the claimed source recorded; it is not independently verified. In deterministic fixtures, supplied business records are KNOWN for that fixture.

ESTIMATED (OWNER ESTIMATE): recalled, approximated or owner-reported figures. State range/basis when available. Derived results from estimates remain ESTIMATED.

ASSUMED (PLUGIN ASSUMPTION): a hypothetical input used to explore sensitivity. Explicitly disclose rationale; never invent industry authority. Owner acceptance does not upgrade it to KNOWN.

POTENTIAL OPPORTUNITY: prospective incremental outcome conditional on the stated interventions, conversion, capacity and input assumptions; never measured lost revenue or a guarantee. Its inputs retain their original labels.

UNKNOWN: not enough information. Leave blank/not assessed rather than treating as zero. Qualitative findings are allowed.

## Confidence applies per claim

HIGH: directly supported by comparable business data. MEDIUM: partly supported by owner estimates. LOW: materially depends on assumptions, unreconciled data or incomplete evidence. Use the weakest material input, not an average of input strengths. Good call logs can justify high confidence in unanswered-call count while recovery opportunity remains low confidence. There is no numeric confidence percentage. Confidence is evidence certainty, not likelihood of implementation success.

## Priority: impact × evidence × fixability, qualitatively

HIGH: meaningful business impact, enough evidence to act, and a practical fix or low-cost validation. Explain why it comes first. MEDIUM: plausible impact with a constraint or evidence gap; validation may precede implementation. LOW: marginal relevance, weak evidence, substantial effort or dependencies; explain deferral. A cheap measurement action can be high priority even when the resulting revenue hypothesis has low confidence. High impact alone cannot justify expensive deployment on weak evidence.

Compare effort, available capacity, accountable owner, dependencies and whether fixing an upstream bottleneck helps downstream stages. No multiplication, weights or point scores. Do not prioritize by dollar value alone or by which fix would sell services.
