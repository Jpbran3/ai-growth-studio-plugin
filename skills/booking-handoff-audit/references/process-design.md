# Build a process the owner can use

Choose the smallest fix addressing the supported cause. A shared availability check and explicit receiving owner may solve a gap without new software.

A usable blueprint specifies suggested role, trigger, minimum information, acceptance condition, exception route, dependency, success measure and review trigger. Label unconfirmed assignments and timings as recommendations.

A possible booking flow, adapted to the business:
1. Confirm service fit, location coverage and customer constraints; use established emergency escalation if relevant.
2. Check a feasible slot against qualified staff, travel, equipment and delivery capacity. Distinguish request, tentative hold and confirmed appointment.
3. Restate agreed service scope, time window and any owner-approved price/fee policy. Do not invent prices or arrival guarantees.
4. Record the next owner and minimum service details. Require the receiving role to acknowledge the handoff; unanswered acceptance returns to the originating owner or agreed exception route.
5. Give the customer owner-approved confirmation details. Draft only unless sending is separately authorized. Respect preferences and explicit declines.
6. Review unmatched handoffs, avoidable reschedules and matured appointment outcomes. Check whether improvement displaces other profitable/deliverable work.

Suggested customer draft: “We can offer [verified slot] for [confirmed service]. Please confirm whether that works for you. [Owner-approved preparation/fee details, if applicable].” Replace placeholders only with supplied facts; omit unsupported details. Do not treat sending the draft as an action taken.

Automation becomes an option when a stable manual process exists and evidence supports the need. Specify the trigger, input source, responsible role, failure escalation, human exceptions and measurement before proposing a tool. An AI agent must not promise unavailable slots or diagnose trade hazards. Do not default to a CRM migration, discounts or software procurement.
