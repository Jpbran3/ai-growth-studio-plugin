# Maintenance Renewal Audit: diagnosis

Journey: existing agreement or documented service schedule → verified due obligation → contact/booking readiness → completed service → eligible renewal decision.

Inspect actual agreement terms or documented owner-approved schedule when available. Do not invent maintenance intervals, contract rights, coverage, auto-renewal authorization, fees or cancellation rules. Separate service owed under an active agreement, optional reminder, eligible renewal and expired/ineligible records. A cancelled plan is not a renewal opportunity by default. Check duplicate assets/accounts, already completed visits, future not-due service, current active bookings and preferences. Record unknown terms as unknown; do not decide obligations from generic industry assumptions.

Find gaps in due-date evidence, next-action ownership, workable availability and acknowledgement between office and field team. Fulfillment of already-paid work is an obligation and operating load, not new revenue. Keep routine planning separate from technical or hazardous service instructions.

Separate symptom, cause hypothesis, alternative explanation and validation step. Labels and limitations belong with findings. Use anonymized examples/aggregates where possible; no credentials or unnecessary customer information. A brief focused report is preferable to repeating unrelated funnel questions.
